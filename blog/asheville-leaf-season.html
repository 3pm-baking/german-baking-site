Asheville Fall Colors: Where the Leaf Season Peaks (and Mary's Mom's First Indian Summer)

October 10, 2026 · William · 3 min read
✿

My mother-in-law is visiting from Germany right now, here for all of October. It is the first time she has seen Mary in America since Mary moved here with her Green Card. She has always been interested in the American Indian Summer, that stretch of warm days after the first cold spell. In German, it is called Altweibersommer, though Mary favors another term for the same stretch: goldener Herbst, the golden autumn. Whether she gets the textbook version of it or not, her mom came at the right month, because the leaves are about to do the thing the Blue Ridge Mountains are famous for.

How the leaves do it is worth knowing before you plan a drive, because Asheville's fall color is not one peak, it is a cascade. The color starts high and works its way down. Late September brings the first golds to the highest points along the Blue Ridge Parkway: Craggy Gardens, Mount Mitchell, Graveyard Fields. By mid-October the slopes are turning. Asheville itself, down at around 2,200 feet, peaks in the last week of October, and the lower foothills around Chimney Rock carry the season into early November. The 2026 forecast from Explore Asheville's fall color watchers expects a stretched season this year, because September ran warm and the trees turn more slowly when it does. For once, the timing works in our favor: whenever your week comes free, something is turning.

For driving, the Blue Ridge Parkway is the obvious answer, and the visitor advice from everyone who does this every year is the same: check the official closure status before you leave. Sections of the Parkway open and close with weather and season, and the NPS page is the only reliable source for it.

After a morning of overlooks, there is exactly one sensible way to end the day, and it is a German habit: Kaffee und Kuchen, coffee and cake in the afternoon. I wrote more about that tradition here, and about why our bakery is named 3pm. A leaf drive in the morning, coffee and cake before the light goes. The plan writes itself.

If you are building a leaf weekend around it, we are at the Black Mountain Tailgate Market on Saturday mornings through the season. Black Mountain sits in the same mountains the Parkway runs along, so the drive and the market stop can happen in one trip. Come by the stand, ask Mary's mom how Germany does autumn. She will tell you, and then she will ask you about the leaves.
