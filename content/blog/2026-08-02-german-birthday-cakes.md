## The Email

A customer in Asheville emailed asking for a chocolate birthday cake. I read it to William, and we looked at each other. We did not have to say it. *Donauwelle*.

That got us talking about how birthdays work in Germany versus here. William knows the traditions, but the differences still come up.

## The Culture Clips That Keep Happening

Two things William knows but his American instincts still take over.

First, he catches himself wishing people happy birthday early. Americans say "happy early birthday" all the time. In Germany, that is bad luck. You wait until the actual day. I keep catching him doing it.

Second, the fact that the birthday person is expected to bring the cake still gets him every time. It is not optional. If it is your birthday, you show up with *Kuchen* to work, to school, to your friends. But you get to bring your favorite, and that is the upside.

## The Classics

So what do people bring? The cakes everyone grew up with.

*Donauwelle* is a marble cake base with sour cherries, vanilla custard buttercream, and a chocolate glaze swirled across the top. It is the cake you suggest when someone wants a chocolate birthday cake. It feeds a crowd and disappears fast.

*Erdbeertorte* is a light vanilla sponge with fresh strawberries and a clear glaze. Maybe the most common birthday cake of all. Simple and bright, especially in summer.

*Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte* is chocolate, cream, and cherries. The classic everyone knows. It is what Germans picture when they think of a celebration cake.

[*Käsekuchen*](/products/german-cheesecake.html) is German cheesecake. Lighter than American cheesecake, tangy from *Quark*. It shows up for people who want something that does not sit heavy.

## Afternoon Coffee

Even a birthday follows the *Kaffee und Kuchen* rhythm. You serve cake in the afternoon with coffee, not at midnight after a meal. That is when people can sit down and enjoy a slice. I wrote more about that tradition [here](/blog/why-3pm-kaffee-und-kuchen.html).
